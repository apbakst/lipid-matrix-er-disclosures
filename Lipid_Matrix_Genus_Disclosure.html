2. FIELD OF THE DISCLOSURE

The disclosure relates to oral, sublingual, buccal, and other solid dosage forms providing extended, sustained, controlled, modified, prolonged, delayed, pulsatile, biphasic, or zero-order release of one or more active pharmaceutical ingredients (“APIs”) from a lipid-containing matrix. The lipid matrix may be the sole release-controlling phase, the dominant release-controlling phase, or one of two or more release-controlling phases in a multiphasic dosage form.

The disclosure is particularly applicable to extended-release oral minoxidil for the treatment of hair loss, but expressly extends to any small-molecule API for which a lipid-matrix extended-release form is therapeutically useful.

3. PROBLEM ADDRESSED

Immediate-release oral minoxidil produces a Cmax that may exceed the cardiac activity threshold (~20 ng/mL), causing tachycardia, hypotension, edema, and other dose-limiting effects. Lipid matrix systems provide an extended-release pathway based on erosion, diffusion, and/or dissolution through hydrophobic and amphiphilic excipients. They are useful for any sparingly water-soluble API requiring reduced peak plasma concentration, sustained therapeutic levels, reduced dosing frequency, improved adherence, reduced food effect, or improved tolerability.

The disclosed compositions may be manufactured by any one of the following processes, or any combination or sequence thereof. All process parameter ranges include endpoints. Equivalent commercial equipment from any vendor is expressly disclosed.

12.1 Melt granulation (preferred)

Heat one or more lipid matrix formers (per §5) to a temperature between 5 °C above the lowest melting point and 30 °C above the highest melting point of the lipid components, but in any case between 35 °C and 200 °C, preferably between 50 °C and 120 °C, most preferably between 65 °C and 95 °C. Mix at any agitation rate from 5 to 1000 rpm. Add API and optionally release modifiers, fillers, and surfactants. Mix between 30 seconds and 4 hours. Cool by any of: passive cooling on trays, active jacketed cooling, cryogenic cooling (CO₂, N₂), forced-air cooling, conveyor belt cooling, drum cooling, or pastillation. Mill the cooled mass through a screen of mesh size 10 to 200 (corresponding to particle sizes ~75 μm to ~2000 μm). Optional annealing at any temperature 5–30 °C below the lowest lipid melting point for 1 minute to 30 days. Equipment: high-shear mixers (Diosna, Glatt VG, Aeromatic-Fielder GP, Loedige), planetary mixers, jacketed kettles, twin-shell blenders.

12.2 Hot-melt extrusion (HME)

Feed pre-blended API + lipid matrix former + optional excipients into a single-screw or twin-screw (co-rotating or counter-rotating, intermeshing or non-intermeshing) extruder with 2 to 16 heating zones. Zone temperatures from 30 °C to 220 °C, screw speed from 5 to 1000 rpm, throughput 0.1 to 500 kg/h. Die configurations: rod, ribbon, strand, lens, sheet, cylinder, multi-hole. Downstream: chill roll, conveyor, pelletizer, cryomill, hammer mill, jet mill. Equipment: Leistritz, Thermo Fisher Pharma 11/16/24, Coperion ZSK series, Steer Omicron, Brabender, Haake, Buss kneader.

12.3 Spray congealing / spray chilling

Atomize molten lipid (containing dissolved or dispersed API) through pressure, two-fluid, ultrasonic, rotary disk, or vibrating-orifice nozzle into a cooled chamber at −40 °C to +30 °C. Particle size 5 μm to 2 mm. Equipment: Buchi B-290 with chilling unit, GEA Niro spray congealer, ProCepT 4M8, Brace pastillator.

12.4 Melt pelletization

In a high-shear granulator at 40 °C to 110 °C, agglomerate lipid + API + excipients to spherical pellets of 0.1 to 3 mm. Optional in-situ extrusion-spheronization with heated equipment.

12.5 Spray drying with lipid carrier

Dissolve or disperse lipid + API in a volatile solvent (ethanol, isopropanol, dichloromethane, ethyl acetate, acetone, tert-butanol, water, or mixtures), spray-dry at inlet 80–220 °C, outlet 40–120 °C, atomization pressure 0.5–10 bar.

12.6 Solvent evaporation / casting

Dissolve lipid + API in a co-solvent system, evaporate under vacuum, ambient, or elevated temperature to form a solid lipid matrix. Mill or compress as needed.

12.7 Supercritical fluid processing

Particles from gas-saturated solutions (PGSS); rapid expansion of supercritical solutions (RESS); supercritical antisolvent (SAS); solution-enhanced dispersion by supercritical fluids (SEDS); aerosol solvent extraction system (ASES). Pressure 50–500 bar, temperature 30–150 °C, CO₂ flow 0.1–100 kg/h.

12.8 Coacervation

Phase separation of lipid + API in solvent + non-solvent system; harden by cooling, cross-linking, or solvent removal.

12.9 Solid lipid nanoparticle / nanostructured lipid carrier preparation

High-pressure homogenization (hot or cold), microemulsion, solvent diffusion, solvent injection, double emulsification, ultrasonication, membrane contactor; pressures 100–2000 bar.

12.10 Cryogenic milling / freeze-grinding

Mill lipid + API at −30 °C to −196 °C using LN₂ or solid CO₂; particle size 1–500 μm.

12.11 Direct compression

Pre-blend API + lipid matrix former (in solid form, with or without prior thermal pretreatment) + fillers + lubricants; compress at 2–50 kN main force on rotary, single-station, or instrumented presses.

12.12 Wet granulation with molten lipid binder

Top-spray, bottom-spray (Wurster), or tangential-spray fluid-bed granulation with molten lipid binder; bed temperature above lipid melting point, atomization pressure 1–6 bar.

12.13 3D printing of lipid matrices

Fused deposition modeling (FDM), semi-solid extrusion (SSE), selective laser sintering (SLS), powder bed fusion, drop-on-powder, drop-on-drop. Layer height 10–500 μm, nozzle temperature 40–200 °C.

12.14 Compression-coating, dry-coating, electrostatic coating

Apply lipid matrix as core or shell over an immediate-release or extended-release inner phase using compression-coating, dry-powder coating, or electrostatic coating equipment.

12.15 Co-melt with simultaneous in-situ complexation

Melt lipid + API in presence of cyclodextrin, mesoporous silica, polymer carrier, or co-former to produce a co-complex within the lipid matrix.

12.16 Equipment equivalence statement

For each process listed, any equivalent commercial or laboratory-scale equipment from any vendor (current or future), and any equivalent process parameter range achieving substantially the same lipid-matrix solid form, is expressly disclosed.

15. RELEASE PROFILE GENUS

The disclosed lipid matrix releases API according to one or more of the following profiles. All are expressly disclosed.

  • Zero-order: linear release, slope ±20% from constant.
  • First-order: exponential decay.
  • Higuchi (square-root-of-time): typical of monolithic matrices.
  • Korsmeyer–Peppas: with release exponent n from 0.30 to 1.00.
  • Hixson–Crowell: cube-root-of-time (erosion-controlled).
  • Biphasic: initial burst (5–40%) followed by zero- or first-order phase.
  • Triphasic: lag + sustained + terminal.
  • Pulsatile: delayed onset followed by burst.
  • pH-dependent: release rate function of GI pH.
  • Site-specific: colonic, ileal, gastric, jejunal targeted.

In vitro dissolution acceptance criteria (USP <711> Apparatus 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, or 7) at any combination of:

  • Medium: pH 1.2 (0.1 N HCl), pH 4.5 (acetate), pH 6.8 (phosphate), pH 7.4 (phosphate), simulated gastric fluid (with/without enzymes), simulated intestinal fluid (with/without enzymes), FaSSGF, FaSSIF (V1, V2, V3), FeSSGF, FeSSIF (V1, V2, V3), milk, biorelevant media, hydroalcoholic media (5–40% ethanol for in-vitro alcohol dose-dumping testing).
  • Volume: 100–4000 mL.
  • Speed: 25–250 rpm (paddle), 50–200 rpm (basket).
  • Temperature: 37.0 ± 0.5 °C.
  • Sampling: 5 min through 48 hours.

Alcohol dose-dumping resistance: lipid matrix compositions disclosed herein are expected to resist accelerated release in 5%, 20%, and 40% ethanol-containing media; this property is expressly disclosed and claimed-to-public-domain.
